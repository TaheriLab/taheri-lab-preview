# Research projects

The Taheri Lab develops machine learning methods to study cancer
biology, predict drug interactions, and identify opportunities
for drug repurposing. Our work combines biological knowledge
with molecular data to investigate complex biomedical questions.


{% include section.html %}

## Cancer biology and multi-omics

We develop machine learning methods to identify genes, regulatory
networks, and molecular pathways involved in cancer development,
progression, and recurrence.

Our research integrates single-cell and other multi-omics data
with biological networks to study differences between tumors
and identify molecular patterns associated with clinical outcomes.

Current projects include breast cancer gene regulation, recurrence
in advanced ovarian cancer, and metastatic behavior in lung cancer.

{% include section.html %}

## Drug interactions, treatment effects, and repurposing

We develop machine learning methods to predict drug–drug
interactions and investigate their biological mechanisms.

By combining information about drugs, molecular targets,
biological pathways, and adverse effects, we aim to improve
drug interaction prediction and identify potential new uses
for existing drugs.

We also analyze cancer cell–fibroblast co-cultures at single-cell
resolution to study how drug treatments affect the spatial
organization of these cell populations.

We collaborate with experimental and clinical researchers at
Karolinska Institutet (KI) on both our cancer biology and
drug research projects.

## Computational methods

Across these research areas, we develop graph-based and multimodal
machine learning methods, integrate biomedical knowledge graphs,
and investigate how biological knowledge can improve predictions
and their interpretation.



## Selected projects

### SciLifeLab and Wallenberg DDLS Fellowship

#### Understanding the molecular basis of lung cancer metastasis

We develop machine learning methods to investigate molecular
features associated with metastatic progression in lung cancer.
By integrating tumor molecular profiles with clinical data,
we study patterns linked to metastatic behavior.

#### Image analysis of drug responses in cancer–fibroblast co-cultures

We develop image-analysis and machine learning methods to study
cancer cell–fibroblast co-cultures at single-cell resolution.
The project examines how drug treatments affect the spatial
organization of these cell populations, aiming to identify
spatial features associated with treatment responses.

Both projects involve collaboration with experimental and
clinical researchers at Karolinska Institutet.

### Swedish Research Council

#### Graph-based learning for ovarian cancer recurrence

We develop graph-based machine learning methods that integrate
biological knowledge with single-cell multi-omics data to study
recurrence in advanced ovarian cancer. The project aims to identify
molecular patterns associated with recurrence and understand the
biological processes underlying differences in patient outcomes.

This project is conducted in collaboration with experimental and
clinical researchers at Karolinska Institutet.

### Digital Futures

#### AI for heterogeneous disease trajectories

*Seed project*

We explore machine learning methods to understand how disease
progression varies across individuals. By combining different types
of health data, the project aims to characterize patient heterogeneity
and identify patterns in disease trajectories.

This project is conducted in collaboration with researchers at KTH.

#### ImpactTwin — Digital twins for infrastructure risk assessment

*Proof of Concept project*

We develop an interactive digital-twin dashboard to assess
infrastructure risks from extreme events. Using flooding as
an initial case study, the project combines spatial data and
impact modelling to help users explore scenarios and inform
planning decisions.

This project is conducted in collaboration with researchers at WaterCentre@KTH.

#### Explainable AI for cancer risk assessment

*Research Pair project*

We develop machine learning methods to assess breast
and prostate cancer risk using Swedish national health registry
data. The project investigates how environmental, lifestyle,
and clinical factors contribute to risk.

This project is conducted in collaboration with researchers at KTH.
